Up or down.
In or out.

A barrier name is two instructions joined together. Decode those two words first; only then ask whether the surviving payoff is a call, a put, a coupon or something else.

The two-word decoder

Up / downWhere is the tripwire?
In / outWhat happens when it is hit?

Direction locates the barrier. In or out controls the switch.

The word “knock” describes a state change, not a cash payment. A knock-in does not pay when touched. It merely makes the underlying payoff eligible to exist later.

Read the contract, not the diagram

A line is not a barrier until the terms define it

Two products can draw the same 80% line yet behave differently because they observe it differently.

  1. 01What is observed?

    Spot, a closing level, an index fixing, a basket member or the worst performer?

  2. 02Where is the level?

    Absolute price, percentage of initial level, or a moving level?

  3. 03When is it observed?

    Continuously, at closes, on scheduled dates, during a window or only at maturity?

  4. 04What changes after touch?

    An option activates, terminates, a note redeems, or a coupon condition changes?

  5. 05Is there a rebate?

    A separate cash amount may be due after a knock-out or an untriggered knock-in.

Common traps

Five distinctions worth keeping separate

Barrier ≠ strike

The barrier changes eligibility. The strike determines the vanilla payoff if the option is active.

Touch ≠ maturity level

A path can touch, recover and finish far away. Path monitoring remembers the earlier event.

Knock-in ≠ instant payout

Touch normally activates a later payoff. That payoff can still finish out of the money.

Autocall trigger ≠ downside barrier

An autocall can redeem the note early, while a separate knock-in governs downside at maturity.

Monitoring ≠ a small detail

An intraday breach may count under continuous monitoring and not count under close-only observation.
