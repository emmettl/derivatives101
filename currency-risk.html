Whose Currency Is the Risk?

A foreign asset can rise while its home-currency value falls. The missing instruction is conversion: use the terminal exchange rate, fix it in advance, or avoid translation altogether.

Specification discipline

“Converted into home currency” is not executable

The legal payoff rule and the pricing model are related but separate. A specification needs exact currency roles, observations and fallbacks.

DecisionQuestion to resolveWhy it matters

Quote direction changes the sign

This lab quotes home currency per unit of foreign currency. If the legal document uses the reciprocal convention, “FX up” means the opposite economic move.

Quanto is not free FX protection

The terminal payoff omits spot FX translation, but the product’s fair value can still depend on domestic and foreign rates, equity volatility, FX volatility and equity–FX correlation.

Payoff mechanics are not pricing

The fixed conversion rate shown here is 1.00 in normalized units. A real transaction’s rate, premium or participation reflects its market and contractual terms.

Currency-payoff references

CME’s FX quote-convention guide identifies the first currency as the base currency and the second as the quote currency, making the direction of an exchange rate explicit. The OCC options disclosure document explains that a currency option relates one currency to another and that contracts may use cash or physical settlement. OCC’s cash-settled currency-option specifications illustrate why the settlement-value source and observation time are product terms, not implementation details. This lab uses normalized fictional indices and does not reproduce any listed contract.
