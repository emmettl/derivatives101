Lab 15 · Forward pricing & carry

Spot Is Not the Forward

An option expiring in the future is priced against a future delivery level, not by pretending today’s spot simply waits there. Financing pushes that level up; expected dividends pull it down.

Scope:A fictional dividend-paying equity with flat, continuously compounded rates and a continuous dividend yield. European options use Black–Scholes in forward form. Taxes, discrete dividends, stock borrow, transaction costs, curve shapes and market bid–offer are excluded.

Specification discipline

Decision Question to resolve Why it matters

A forward is not a forecast

It is a delivery price implied by spot and carry assumptions. Expected future spot under a real-world view is a different quantity.

Dividend risk is a pricing input

Replacing a dated dividend schedule with one flat yield is convenient, not neutral. Amounts, ex-dates and tax treatment can matter.

Carry moves product terms

A fixed call budget buys less participation when carry makes the relevant call richer, and more when the call becomes cheaper.

Forward and carry references

CME’s equity-index fair-value guide describes theoretical futures value using the cash index, interest, dividends and time to expiry. Its equity-index basis lesson explains why financing and dividend income can place futures above or below spot. The Options Industry Council’s option-price guidance identifies spot, strike, tenor, rates, dividend yield and volatility as pricing inputs, while its implied-forward lesson connects call–put relationships to the market-implied forward. This lab uses a simplified continuous-yield model rather than any listed contract or market dataset.
