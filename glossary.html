Glossary

Plain-language definitions for the terms reused across lessons, payoff diagrams and lifecycle labs.

How to use itSearch by term or ideaCoverageOptions, barriers, baskets and risk

Autocall

Automatic early redemption when a published underlying test is met on an observation date. Unlike an issuer call, it is triggered by the contract rather than discretion.

Barrier

A level whose observation changes a payoff. The contract must also say when and how it is observed: continuously, at daily closes, on scheduled dates or only at maturity. Explore barrier mechanics →

Barrier option

An option that starts or stops existing when its underlying reaches a barrier. Knock-in and knock-out options are the two broad forms. Compare all four mechanisms →

Bisection

A numerical search that tests the midpoint of a range, keeps the half containing the target and repeats. It is dependable when the model output moves consistently with the unknown input.

Bond floor

The present value of a promised maturity repayment. In a capital-protected note, the issue proceeds left after buying the floor can fund options.

Bonus certificate

A participation product with a conditional minimum redemption. If its barrier is breached, the bonus floor disappears and the product normally becomes tracker-like.

Call option

A contract giving the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy an underlying at the strike. The seller has the corresponding obligation.

Callable

Redeemable early at the issuer’s discretion under stated terms. “Callable” and “autocallable” therefore identify different owners of the decision.

Capital protection

A contractual floor on redemption, usually applying only at maturity and only while the issuer can pay. It does not mean the note cannot trade below par earlier.

Carry

The effect of funding, interest, dividends and related holding costs over time. Carry helps determine a forward price and is not itself a forecast.

Conditional coupon

A coupon paid only if an underlying test passes on the observation date. The coupon barrier can differ from the principal protection barrier.

Coupon

An interest payment on a note, quoted as a percentage of notional. Its frequency, conditions and annualisation convention matter as much as the headline rate.

Correlation

A measure of how closely assets move together. Lower correlation tends to create more dispersion, which can worsen the weakest member of a worst-of basket.

Delta

The approximate change in an option or product value for a one-unit change in the underlying, holding other assumptions constant.

Discount certificate

Underlying exposure bought below spot in exchange for giving up gains above a cap. Economically it resembles a covered call or a reverse convertible paid up front.

Dispersion

The degree to which basket constituents spread apart. A worst-of investor effectively sells exposure to a weak outlier, so more dispersion can reduce redemption.

Fair value

A model estimate of the present economic value under stated market inputs and assumptions. It is a reference point, not necessarily an executable price, and changes when the model or inputs change.

Forward

A price agreed today for delivery at a future date. Forwards are linked to spot by no-arbitrage carry, not by a directional market prediction.

Gamma

The rate at which delta changes as the underlying moves. High gamma means a hedge needs to be adjusted more quickly.

Gearing

A multiplier applied to exposure. In an accumulator, the daily quantity commonly increases below the strike; in leveraged products it magnifies returns and losses.

Implied volatility

The volatility consistent with an option’s market price under a model. It is the market price of uncertainty, not a prediction of direction.

Issuer credit risk

The risk that the product issuer cannot meet its obligations. Most structured notes are unsecured claims, including those described as capital protected.

Knock-in

A barrier event that activates an option or downside mechanism. In many notes, principal loss still also requires a weak final underlying level.

Knock-out

A barrier event that terminates an option or product feature. In accumulators it usually caps a favourable path by stopping future daily trades.

Local volatility

A path model in which the volatility used for the next move depends on the current underlying level and time. The course’s illustrative downside-skew model uses higher local volatility below the initial level and lower volatility above it.

Memory coupon

A conditional coupon whose missed amounts are banked and can be paid later if a future observation passes. Memory affects coupon timing, not principal protection.

Mini future

A leveraged certificate whose value is approximately the gap between spot and a financing level. A stop-loss ends it before that gap becomes adverse.

Notional

The reference amount used to calculate a payoff. It can be much larger than cash initially paid or margin posted.

Observation date

A scheduled date on which a price or condition is tested. Different product features may use different observation calendars.

Participation rate

The multiplier applied to an underlying gain. In a protected note it is the available option budget divided by the cost of one unit of the option.

Physical settlement

Settlement by delivering the underlying rather than paying an equivalent cash amount. Share counts, board lots, holidays and corporate actions then matter.

Premium

The price paid by an option buyer to an option seller. For a product investor selling optionality, premium can appear as coupon, discount or another enhanced term.

Put option

A contract giving the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to sell an underlying at the strike. A short put can create equity-like downside.

Reverse convertible

An issuer note plus a short put. The investor receives an enhanced coupon and accepts conditional downside in principal.

Spot

The price for immediate ownership or settlement of an asset, used as the starting reference for many strikes and barriers.

Solver tolerance

The maximum difference allowed between a model result and its target before a numerical solve is treated as complete. A tighter tolerance usually requires more iterations and does not make the model itself more accurate.

Stop-loss

A level that terminates a mini future. The residual value depends on execution and market gaps; it is not always zero.

Strike

The contractual price at which an option can be exercised or at which shares may be delivered. It is fixed by the terms, not reset by later market moves unless explicitly stated.

Theta

The change in option value caused by time passing, with other assumptions held constant.

Vega

The change in option value for a change in implied volatility. An investor who has sold options is commonly short vega.

Volatility

A measure of how much an asset moves, not which way. Higher expected volatility generally increases option premium.

Volatility skew

The pattern of different implied volatilities across strikes. Equity markets commonly price downside strikes at higher volatility than at-the-money strikes, which can materially change barrier-product values.

Worst-of

A basket rule that uses the weakest-performing constituent. Adding another name can never improve the worst observation and generally raises product risk.
