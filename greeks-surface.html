Lab 21 · Risk you can see

Greeks Surface Explorer

A barrier reverse convertible looks calm for most of its life. Near the barrier, close to maturity, its risk numbers stop behaving: delta jumps, gamma spikes and vega can change sign. Here the whole map of those numbers is drawn as one surface you can turn around, against where the stock is and how long the note has left to run.

Scope:One-year note on a single stock, strike 100%, knock-in put with no rebate. Black-Scholes with flat volatility, no dividends, no skew, no issuer funding spread or margin; the coupon is paid at maturity and set so the note is worth par at issue. Greeks are closed-form prices bumped by finite differences. The surface stops one trading day before maturity. Illustrative only.

For developers

How the surface is drawn

The numbers are computed once per change, in double precision on the CPU, on a 321 by 97 grid with time rows bunched towards maturity. The GPU then draws about 61,000 triangles: the vertex shader places each grid point, and the fragment shader lights it and draws the grid, barrier, strike, time slice and marker per pixel, so those lines stay sharp at any angle.

  1. PriceClosed form per grid point, both branches
  2. BumpCentral differences for delta, gamma and vega
  3. MeshHeights, normals and colours into one buffer
  4. DrawWebGPU render pass, 4× multisampled
  5. Fall backSame mesh through WebGL, then a heat map

Why the barrier is not a floor

Far from maturity the surface is smooth: the market prices the chance of a knock-in, not the knock-in itself. As time runs out that chance becomes all or nothing, and all of the note's risk crowds into a narrow band just above the barrier.

Why the desk cares

The issuer hedges the put it bought by trading the stock in proportion to delta. Where gamma spikes, that hedge has to be rebalanced in large size on small moves, which is costly and is why barrier levels and observation styles are priced carefully.

Observation changes the shape

A continuously monitored barrier knocks in the moment it is touched, so its risk sits just above it. A barrier checked only at maturity leaves a cliff in the payoff, and the cliff's risk straddles the barrier on both sides.

Method references

The continuously monitored put is the Reiner-Rubinstein down-and-in formula; once spot is at or below the barrier it is the Black-Scholes put. Daily-close monitoring shifts the barrier by the Broadie-Glasserman-Kou correction, B·exp(−0.5826·σ·√Δt). The at-maturity barrier is a put struck at the barrier plus a cash-or-nothing digital paying strike minus barrier. The normal distribution uses Hart's double-precision algorithm as given by West (2005), so second differences stay clean. Drawing follows the W3C WebGPU and WGSL specifications.
