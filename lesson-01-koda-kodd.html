Lesson 01 · Path dependency

KODA & KODD

A discounted daily trade that can grow into a large obligation precisely when the market moves against the client.

In one sentence

A contract to keep buying, a little every day, at a discount

In a knock-out discount accumulator, the client must buy a fixed quantity on every scheduled observation at a strike below the initial spot. Below that strike, the quantity commonly doubles. A rally through the knock-out ends the trade; a fall keeps it alive and increases the size bought.

The favourable outcome is short and capped. The adverse outcome is long, geared and cumulative.
Obligation

The client must transact; this is not a right they can abandon.

Discount

The below-spot strike is funded by option premium, not given away.

Gearing

The daily amount increases on the adverse side of the strike.

Knock-out

A favourable move terminates future participation.

Why the path matters

The knock-out is a trapdoor, not a target

The whole contract is a one-day obligation repeated many times. The sequence matters because each fixing creates another purchase. An early rally can stop accumulation with a small gain. A prolonged fall keeps creating purchases, often at the geared quantity, so exposure is largest when the client is most wrong.

Term-sheet anatomy

Three numbers expose the real size

First calculate maximum units: scheduled observations multiplied by base quantity and gearing. Then multiply by the strike to get maximum exposure. This is not the same as the margin posted. Finally, compare the strike-to-knock-out window with the downside that remains below the strike.

Guaranteed observations

Fixings that continue even if the knock-out has already been reached.

Settlement frequency

Daily fixings may accumulate into monthly physical deliveries.

Margin is the practical risk that often ends the position first. The mark-to-market reflects remaining optionality and future geared purchases, not only the loss already visible in delivered shares.

Takeaways

What to remember

It is an obligation

“Buy at a discount” omits the repeated mandatory purchase.

Path dominates endpoint

Two paths ending at the same price can create different quantities.

Gearing changes loss, not gain

It increases the adverse purchase rate without lifting the knock-out profit.

Size the maximum exposure

Margin posted is not the economic notional.
