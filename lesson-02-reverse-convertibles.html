Reverse convertibles

A high coupon paid for with conditional principal: bond plus short put, with barriers and early redemption layered on top.

In one sentence

A high coupon, paid for with your principal

A reverse convertible pays an above-market coupon and repays par only while its protection condition survives. Economically, the investor owns an issuer bond and sells a put on the underlying. If the short put finishes in the money, principal follows the asset down.

The coupon is fixed in advance. The principal is the uncertain part.
Vanilla form

Principal falls one-for-one whenever the underlying ends below the strike.

Barrier form

The put activates only if the knock-in condition is met, usually plus a weak final level.

Two separate tests

Coupon and principal barriers price different risks

A coupon barrier asks whether one coupon is paid on one date. A protection barrier asks whether principal can be lost. Memory may defer a missed coupon; it does not repair principal protection. Read the two schedules separately.

A barrier breach can be necessary but not sufficient for loss. In many notes, the underlying must both breach during the life and finish below the strike. A recovery can therefore save principal without undoing the historical breach.

Who decides when it ends?

Callable and autocallable are not synonyms

Callable

The issuer holds the right to redeem. It will exercise when doing so benefits the issuer.

Autocallable

A published underlying test automatically redeems the note when met.

A step-down autocall schedule becomes easier to trigger over time. That improves the probability of early repayment but caps participation in a recovery. The client cannot choose to keep the attractive coupon alive.

Worst-of adds another dimension

With several underlyings, the weakest name drives the relevant tests. Adding a name raises coupon because it lowers the expected payout. The extra yield and the extra risk are the same fact seen from opposite sides.

Keep the tests separate

A daily knock-in barrier was breached, but the underlying recovered above the strike by maturity. What usually happens?

Takeaways

What to remember

Bond plus short put

The coupon spreads the option premium over time.

Conditions are independent

Coupon, call and protection tests can all use different levels.

Callability has an owner

Issuer choice and automatic level tests have different economics.

Worst-of sells dispersion

More names increase both coupon and the chance of a weak outlier.
