Discount certificates

Buy below spot, give up everything above the cap

A discount certificate is economically a covered call: exposure to the underlying plus a short call. It is also a reverse convertible paid up front: the option premium appears as a lower issue price instead of a coupon. The discount is a certain cushion, but upside ends at the cap.

Same option, different packaging: a discount certificate and a reverse convertible can carry the same maturity risk.
Certain discount

The initial purchase price is below the current underlying level.

Certain cap

Every outcome above the cap redeems at the same maximum amount.

Bonus certificates

A floor only while the barrier survives

A bonus certificate keeps open-ended upside and offers a minimum redemption at the bonus level, provided the barrier is never breached under the stated observation rule. After breach, the floor disappears permanently and the certificate tracks the underlying—usually without passing through dividends.

Where the feature comes from

The client pays for the bonus with their own dividends

A tracker can be represented by a zero-strike call. Because a certificate holder normally does not receive the underlying’s dividends, the present value of those dividends becomes the feature budget. Higher-dividend names can fund a higher bonus, a deeper barrier or greater upside participation.

Barrier discount

A larger conditional cushion than a plain discount, but it can vanish after a touch.

Outperformance

The same dividend budget is directed upward to buy extra call participation.

Takeaways

What to remember

Discount means short call

The certain cushion is exchanged for a certain upside cap.

Bonus is conditional

One barrier touch can permanently remove the floor.

Dividends are the budget

Foregone income pays for the product feature.

Observation style matters

A maturity-only barrier is safer than continuous monitoring on otherwise identical terms.
