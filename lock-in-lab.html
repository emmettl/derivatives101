Lock-In Mechanics

“Lock-in” is a family of mechanisms, not one universal payoff. Compare a barrier reverse convertible whose par redemption becomes secured with a participation note whose minimum maturity payment ratchets upward.

Scope:Single underlying and discrete observations, with a choice of zero-drift flat volatility or the illustrative downside local-vol model from the Volatility Skew lab. Physical delivery applies only to unprotected downside in the par lock-in variant and uses 100 nominal ÷ strike 100 = 1.000 unit; the step-up variant is cash-settled.

Par lock-in

Terms

One market, changing floor

What became irrevocable?

A lock-in changes the minimum future redemption. Unlike an autocall, the note remains alive and later market levels can still matter.

Path outcome

Two thousand markets

How often does the floor improve?

A lock-in can be valuable without being triggered in every market, and a partial floor may still leave principal at risk.

Persistent state

Observation ledger

Once the floor rises, a later fall does not reverse it. The final redemption compares the ordinary payoff with the highest locked floor.

Date Underlying Lock-in test New floor Coupon paid Note state

Not early redemption

The product remains outstanding after a lock-in event. Later observations and maturity still occur.

Par lock-in

A qualifying observation secures redemption of 100 at maturity, even if a later sell-off would otherwise activate downside.

Step-up minimum

Qualifying observations ratchet a minimum maturity payment upward. The final payment is the greater of that floor and the ordinary participation payoff.

Mechanics references

Published “lock-in” structures use materially different rules. This lab therefore labels its two illustrative conventions explicitly rather than treating lock-in as a universal payoff.
