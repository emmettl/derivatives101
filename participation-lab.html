Outperformance & Bonus

A bonus spends the dividend budget on a conditional floor. Outperformance spends it on extra upside. The combined product tries to buy both—and the terms reveal how that budget was divided.

Scope:Normalised spot, strike and issue price of 100; a single underlying; European maturity; continuous monitoring represented by daily closes. Dividends, fees and volatility feed an illustrative zero-rate option budget, not a tradable price. The separate path simulation can compare flat volatility with the downside local-vol model from the Volatility Skew lab. Taxes, funding spreads, issuer credit, secondary-market value and corporate actions are omitted.

Did the barrier survive?

Both paths finish at exactly the same level. One stays above the barrier; the other touches it and recovers. Switch to maturity-only monitoring and the mid-life touch is deliberately forgotten.

One payoff, two possible states

What survives a barrier breach?

A plain bonus becomes a tracker after breach. A bonus-outperformance certificate loses only its floor: disproportionate upside above the strike remains.

The breach changes state

The historical low and the final level are separate data. Once a continuously monitored bonus barrier is touched, a later recovery does not restore the floor.

1330 does not become a tracker

After breach, a bonus-outperformance certificate becomes an outperformance certificate. Its leveraged upside remains; only the conditional minimum redemption disappears.

One budget, two purchases

Forgone dividends fund extra call exposure, barrier-dependent protection, or both. Stronger terms consume more of the same finite budget.

Mechanics references

The product-state distinctions follow the SSPA Swiss Derivative Map: 1310 reflects downside one-for-one and disproportionate upside; 1320 becomes a tracker after breach; 1330 becomes an outperformance certificate. SIX’s worked explanation also shows why combining the bonus and outperformance features generally weakens each headline term. Exact settlement and observation language remains product-specific.
