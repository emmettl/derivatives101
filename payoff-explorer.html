A knock-out discount accumulator obliges the client to buy a fixed quantity every trading day at the strike, with the quantity geared up below the strike, until a knock-out barrier ends the trade. The left chart is a single day. The right chart is what 2,000 simulated markets do to the whole contract.

Simulation: daily lognormal steps, zero drift, knock-out tested on the daily close, a guaranteed minimum of 20 accumulation days, and the position marked at the final close.

A reverse convertible pays a coupon and repays par unless the underlying breaches a knock-in barrier. Add an autocall trigger and the note usually dies early. Watch what happens to the "probability of loss" figure as you drag the barrier and the volatility.

Terms

Autocall is tested on each observation date after a one-period non-call window; coupons are paid up to redemption. No issuer credit event is modelled.

An Asian fixed coupon note pays its coupon whatever happens, and settles into the worst-performing share if that share is both through the barrier and below the strike. Add names to the basket and drop the correlation — then watch the loss probability move while the coupon stays exactly where you set it.

Terms

Knock-in observed daily on each name; autocall observed monthly after the first month, and requires every name to be at or above the trigger. Settlement is into the worst performer at the strike.

A discount certificate is bought below spot and capped at the cap level. There is no barrier on the plain version — the cushion is the discount, and it is small but certain. Add a barrier and the cushion becomes large but conditional.

A bonus certificate pays at least the bonus level if the barrier survives, and participates 1:1 above it. It is funded by the dividends the client gives up — so push the dividend slider and watch how much bonus the budget can actually buy.

The dividend yield is the budget that funds the bonus. It is also what the client forgoes — including after a breach, which is why a breached bonus certificate underperforms the share itself.

A capital protected note buys a bond floor first and spends what is left on options. Drag the interest rate slider to the left and watch the product become unbuildable — that is the whole story of 2015 to 2021 in one control.

Black-Scholes on an at-the-money call, annual compounding on the bond floor. Illustrative arithmetic for teaching, not a pricing tool — and it ignores issuer credit entirely, which is the risk that actually matters.

A mini future costs the gap between spot and the financing level, and dies if the stop-loss is touched. The financing level accretes daily, so for a long the buffer erodes even in a flat market — the carry is charged through the strike, not as a fee.
