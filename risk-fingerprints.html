Interpretation rules

What the numbers do—and do not—say

Greeks are model-based local estimates. Finite shocks reveal some nonlinearity, but neither replaces full scenarios or legal payoff analysis.

Measure Definition in this lab Common trap

Delta is not participation

A 119% maturity participation rate does not mean today’s delta is 1.19. Delta also depends on spot, volatility, dividends and time.

Short options reverse signs

The reverse convertible’s short put generally creates negative gamma and vega. Higher volatility increases the option liability.

Local is not global

Delta predicts a very small move. A ten-point shock also contains gamma and other interactions, so its result need not equal ten times delta.

Risk references

The Options Industry Council’s Greeks overview describes delta, gamma, theta and vega as theoretical guideposts rather than guaranteed price changes. This SEC-filed pricing supplement lists spot, volatility, dividends, remaining time, interest rates and issuer creditworthiness among the interrelated factors that can influence a note before maturity.
