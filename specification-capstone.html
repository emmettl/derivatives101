    What leaves the analysis desk?

    A specification is a connected set of artefacts. Each one answers a different implementation or review question.

    01

    Decision log

    Chosen convention, alternatives considered, owner, rationale and effective product version.

    02

    Normalized terms

    Inputs, data source, dates, operators, schedules, units, rounding and outputs.

    03

    State model

    Allowed states, persistent fields, event ordering, transitions and terminal conditions.

    04

    Worked examples

    At least one ordinary path, a same-day collision and every settlement branch.

    05

    Acceptance tests

    Equality, just-above, just-below, disrupted, repeated and post-termination cases.

    06

    Scope & lineage

    Explicit exclusions, source document version, approvals and traceability from clause to test.

    Completeness

    Every required decision has a value. “Market convention” and product names do not count as values.

    Consistency

    The selected rules can operate together. Physical settlement, for example, must identify a deliverable and ratio.

    Evidence

    Worked traces and boundary tests demonstrate that the prose, algorithm and expected cash flows mean the same thing.

    Why this discipline matters

    The SEC’s structured-notes bulletin highlights payoff formulas, reference assets, barriers, call features, issuer credit and liquidity as terms investors need to understand. FINRA’s investor guidance likewise stresses that structured-note terms can differ significantly and points readers to the prospectus and pricing supplement. For product governance, FINRA Notice 05-26 describes clear, specific and practical review guidelines, while Notice 12-03 discusses the difficulty of understanding novel or complicated derivative-like features. This fictional capstone turns those general concerns into a simplified analysis workflow; it is not legal drafting guidance.
