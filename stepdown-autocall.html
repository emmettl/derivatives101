Lab 14 · Step-down autocall mechanics

A Lower Bar Is Easier to Hit

A step-down autocall does not improve the market path. It lowers the future early-redemption test, making it possible for a note to terminate while the underlying is still below its initial level.

Scope:One fictional underlying starts at 100, with eight quarterly observations. The call threshold steps down before maturity; coupon and maturity-protection tests remain separate. Coupons are non-memory and evaluated before the call test. Figures are cash flows per 100 nominal, not prices or forecasts.

Equality is a business rule

The selected convention is “greater than or equal to.” Exactly on the call threshold redeems; one hundredth below does not.

Label decoder

Specification discipline

Turn the staircase into testable data

An implementation needs a dated schedule and explicit event ordering—not a starting level and an informal decrement.

DecisionQuestion to resolveWhy it matters

Step-down increases callability

As the call threshold falls, progressively weaker market levels can terminate the note. That can shorten the life and remove later coupon opportunities.

Early redemption is not protection

If no call occurs, the separate maturity barrier still determines whether principal is repaid or exposed one-for-one to the final decline.

Worst-of changes the observed level

For a multi-asset note, the least-performing component is commonly tested against the schedule. A strong asset does not compensate for a weak one unless the terms say so.

Step-down references

An SEC-filed pricing supplement illustrates a dated call schedule descending from 100% to 82.5% of initial value. A separate SEC-filed step-down contingent-coupon example distinguishes the call value, coupon barrier and downside threshold and states that no further payments remain after an automatic call. An SEC-filed stepdown snowball example shows that the worst-performing measure can be called at a below-initial final call value while a still-lower threshold governs maturity loss.
